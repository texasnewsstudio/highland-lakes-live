Why Marble Falls Isn’t Named for Marble

The city’s name reaches back to river rapids and a rock that early observers thought resembled marble.

A name from the river

Marble Falls has a name that naturally suggests two things: polished stone and a waterfall. The story is more interesting. The city was named for falls on the Colorado River that once flowed over exposed rock near the present-day community. The stone was not commercial marble in the usual sense; the name reflected how the rock appeared to early observers.

Before the reservoirs

The Colorado River of the nineteenth century looked very different from the chain of lakes familiar today. Rapids and rocky stretches influenced travel, settlement and the way people described the landscape. The falls that gave Marble Falls its name were eventually submerged when Lake Marble Falls was created as part of the region’s dam system.

A city that grew beyond its name

Marble Falls developed as a Hill Country center for commerce, services and visitors. Its downtown, riverfront setting and proximity to nearby lakes made it a natural gathering place. The city’s name remains a link to a physical feature that many present-day residents have never seen in its original form.

Look beyond the obvious

The next time you cross the river or walk through downtown, the name offers a useful reminder: place names are often historical snapshots. They preserve descriptions, hopes and memories from earlier generations even after the terrain has changed. Marble Falls is a particularly good example of a town whose identity still reflects the river that helped shape it.
