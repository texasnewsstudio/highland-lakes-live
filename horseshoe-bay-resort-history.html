How Horseshoe Bay Became a Resort Community

A lakeside development grew into a recognized Hill Country destination with golf, boating and residential neighborhoods.

The appeal of Lake LBJ

Horseshoe Bay occupies a scenic part of the Highland Lakes region along Lake Lyndon B. Johnson. The lake’s generally stable recreational water levels, nearby granite hills and convenient access to Central Texas helped make the area attractive for leisure and residential development.

A resort vision takes shape

Beginning in the latter part of the twentieth century, developers promoted Horseshoe Bay as a destination for golf, lake recreation and second homes. Resort amenities, neighborhoods and marinas gradually gave the community a recognizable identity. Its appeal was not just a single attraction but the combination of water, golf and a quieter Hill Country setting.

From getaway to hometown

Over time, Horseshoe Bay became more than a weekend retreat. Full-time residents, local businesses and civic institutions contributed to a community with year-round needs and traditions. The city incorporated in 2005, an important step in its evolution from a resort-centered development into an incorporated municipality.

A distinctive local identity

Today, visitors may first notice the golf courses, lakeside homes and resort facilities, but the broader story is one of regional growth. Horseshoe Bay reflects the way the Highland Lakes have attracted both recreation and permanent settlement. Its history also offers a useful lens on the changing economy and character of the Texas Hill Country.
