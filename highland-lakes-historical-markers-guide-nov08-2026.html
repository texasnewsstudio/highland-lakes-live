A ROAD TRIP THROUGH THE HIGHLAND LAKES HISTORICAL MARKERS

From old settlements to unusual landmarks, historical markers offer a slower way to discover the region beyond its lake views.

November 8, 2026

It is possible to drive across the Highland Lakes for years and never stop to read the small signs along the road. Yet historical markers can reveal how communities formed, what people once built and why certain places still carry unusual names.

A regional guide to historical-marker road trips recommends beginning with the Texas Historical Commission’s online atlas. That approach makes sense because markers are scattered across the countryside, and not every location has convenient parking or public access.

The atlas can help travelers identify markers that match their interests, whether those involve early settlements, historic buildings or dramatic episodes in local history. It is worth checking descriptions and access details before building an itinerary.

One caution deserves emphasis: some markers are on or near private property. A historical designation does not grant visitors permission to cross fences or enter buildings. Use public roads and designated access areas, and obtain permission when needed.

These stops can also support small-town tourism. A marker visit often leads to a downtown lunch, a museum or a second destination nearby. The result is a more meaningful trip than simply driving from one scenic overlook to another.
