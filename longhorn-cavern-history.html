The Remarkable History of Longhorn Cavern

From underground geology to New Deal-era improvements, Longhorn Cavern is one of the Hill Country’s most unusual landmarks.

A different kind of Hill Country landmark

Not every spectacular Texas landscape is visible from the road. Longhorn Cavern State Park, near Burnet, invites visitors beneath the surface into a network of passages shaped by water and geologic forces. The cave’s smooth contours and dramatic chambers give it a character quite different from the hills and lakes above.

Nature created the setting

Over immense periods, moving water helped shape passages through the rock. Different sections reveal how underground environments can be altered by erosion and changing water conditions. The result is a place where geology becomes tangible: walls, ceilings and corridors tell a story that began long before modern settlement.

People added another chapter

Longhorn Cavern has a human history as well as a natural one. In the 1930s, Civilian Conservation Corps workers helped develop park facilities and improve access as part of New Deal-era public works. Their labor made the cavern more accessible to visitors and left a legacy still associated with the park.

Visiting with curiosity

Guided cave tours allow visitors to learn about both the underground environment and stories associated with the site. Tour offerings, accessibility, hours and admission may change, so check the official operator before traveling. For families, history enthusiasts and geology fans, Longhorn Cavern offers a memorable contrast to a day spent on the water. It is a reminder that some of the Highland Lakes region’s most remarkable scenery lies underground.
