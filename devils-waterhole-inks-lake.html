The Secrets of Devil’s Waterhole

A rocky swimming cove at Inks Lake State Park combines striking geology, Hill Country scenery and a memorable name.

A distinctive corner of Inks Lake

Inks Lake State Park is a favorite Central Texas destination for camping, hiking and enjoying the water. One of its best-known features is Devil’s Waterhole, a scenic inlet framed by large rock outcrops. The contrast between the water, exposed stone and surrounding vegetation makes it one of the most photographed settings in the park.

The rocks tell a longer story

The Highland Lakes sit within a region shaped by immense spans of geologic time. Around Inks Lake, hard ancient rocks and later weathering have produced an especially rugged landscape. Cracks, rounded surfaces and boulders create the unusual contours visitors notice along the shoreline. The setting looks dramatic, but it is the product of natural processes rather than a single sudden event.

A place for exploration

Visitors come to the park to hike, paddle, swim where permitted and enjoy the views. Devil’s Waterhole is reached by park trails, and the terrain can be rocky and uneven. Water conditions, access rules and swimming guidance can change, so checking Texas Parks and Wildlife information before visiting is a good idea. Cliff jumping and climbing can carry serious risks; a scenic photograph should never be treated as proof that a spot is safe.

Why it endures

The name may sound mysterious, but the appeal is easy to understand: a secluded-looking cove, memorable rock formations and a strong sense of place. For residents of Burnet County and visitors from farther away, Devil’s Waterhole illustrates how the Highland Lakes offer much more than open-water recreation. The landscape itself is part of the attraction.
